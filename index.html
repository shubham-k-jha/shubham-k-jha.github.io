Short notes on mistakes my own projects nearly made. Every figure comes from the linked repos.

When the model finds your formulaSolar active regions: a score that jumped from 0.47 to 0.93

In my solar active-region study, adding one feature, emergence duration, lifted cross-validated R² from 0.465 to 0.932. It looked like a discovery. It wasn't. Maximum magnetic flux is almost exactly emergence rate multiplied by duration, and the rank correlation between them is 0.999982. The model was recovering a definition, not learning physics.

One correlation check caught it, with no machine learning involved. So before I read any feature importance, I now ask whether a feature is just a rearrangement of the target or of another feature. Then I ablate. For flare productivity, maximum flux alone (CV R² 0.5435) beat the full feature set (0.4051). Fewer inputs, a better score and an explanation I can defend.

Half the order lines lose moneySuperstore: why a healthy margin can hide a loss problem

Superstore earns a 10.2% margin overall, yet 4,264 of its 8,399 order lines (50.8%) lose money. The average hides the problem, so the useful question is where the losses sit. Tables lose ₹99K and Bookcases ₹34K, while Telephones, Office Machines and Binders each earn more than ₹300K.

Discounting is the other lever. Orders discounted 0 to 10% keep margins of roughly 10 to 13%, and the 11 to 30% group falls to about -21.3%. I calculated that as a dollar-weighted ratio, total profit over total sales, because averaging row-level percentages lets tiny orders distort the result. Low-priority orders also ship in 4.24 days against about 1.5 for everything else, across all three shipping modes, which points to an internal queue rather than a carrier. That is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

Say what the data can't proveMissing is not zero, and a good score is not a good model

Three habits I rely on, each from a different project. First, missing is not zero: in the Netflix Top 10 data, view counts exist for only 62.77% of global records, and not appearing in the Top 10 does not mean nobody watched. Second, respect the end of the window: titles still active in the final week are right-censored, so their observed lifetime is a lower bound.

Third, a strong number can still mislead. My Online Retail cancellation model reaches 0.8448 ROC-AUC, but at the chosen threshold precision is 0.3163 and recall 0.3016. Quantity, price and revenue may also be recorded at or after the event, which can leak the label. So the repo calls it a benchmark, not an early-warning system, and the next version uses a strict prediction cutoff. In Netflix, the forecast uses a chronological 80/20 split rather than a random one, which is why its 84.38% MAE improvement over the naive baseline is believable.
